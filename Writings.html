The way in which people tried abuse this rule was to make so many arguments that the opponent simply did not have enough time to argue against them back. In a classical sonata there are specific rules for the composition, it must be three or four movements, if it is three then the first movement must be in sonata form which has three sections; exposition, development and recapitulation. The exposition must have two themes and the second theme must be modulated to in the dominant key. The development section develops the themes from the exposition and modulates to other fringe keys. The recapitulation brings back the themes from the exposition both in the tonic key and first movements are generally fast. The second movement is a slow movement typically in ternary form. If it’s a four movement sonata then the third movement would be a scherzo and trio or menuet and trio which are written in triple time and often have a dance-like or light-hearted quality.
